The agent platform
Helm

Agents by role. Orchestrated by Flow.

Agents are organized by role, one per function, always on, each tuned to its own KPIs, authority and vocabulary. Flow, inside Helm, chains them into end-to-end value streams.

Inside Helm

Agents

Who owns this function?

  • Always on: watches even when no process runs
  • Conversational: you talk to it directly
  • Acts within defined authority limits
  • Persistent memory across every interaction
Flow · orchestration engine

How does work move across them?

  • Fires on a trigger and runs to completion
  • Spans HR, Legal, Finance and Ops in one workflow
  • Approval gates exactly where they matter
  • Every step logged and traceable

Why this matters: Flow does not ship its own agents. Hire to Retire and Incident to Resolution both call the same HR agent: one set of role knowledge, one authority model, one audit trail. No duplicated logic, and no drift between the agent you talk to and the agent that executes.

Architecture

From the question
to the system of record.

One request, traced end to end. Down through the agent that owns the decision, into the system that owns the record, and back, with a name against every write.

Request descends Answer and audit return Foundation feeds every layer

Governance is the boundary, not a layer at the bottom. Classification, authorisation, PII masking, model eligibility and audit apply at every layer inside the frame, and the whole system ships in the deployment topology your risk posture requires.

Model-agnostic

Models are routed per task. No single vendor is load-bearing, and substitution does not touch the layers above.

Cloud-agnostic

Your cloud or ours. The capability layers are identical either way; only the topology changes.

Governed

Approval gates where they matter and an audit trail on every write. Governance is designed in, not added after.

Deployment

Five ways to run it.
  • SaaS: multi-tenant, fastest to production
  • Dedicated tenant: isolated instance, managed by us
  • Client cloud: your AWS, Azure or GCP account
  • Hybrid: reasoning in cloud, sensitive data held on your side
  • Private on-premises: entirely inside your perimeter

Security

What happens to a request.
  • Data classification: sensitivity determined before anything moves
  • Authorisation: checked against the role, not the requester's intent
  • PII masking: identifiers removed before the model sees the payload
  • Model eligibility: only models cleared for that classification are reachable
  • Output audit: response and action recorded against agent and approver

The economics

Your second hire starts
where the first finished.

Nobody expects the second employee to arrive already knowing the job. Digital ones do. Your roster shares capability, so every agent after the first is faster to put to work and already proven in production.

Build a capability once.
Every agent after that already has it.

A digital employee isn't a monolith. It's a set of discrete capabilities: retrieve this, compare against that, generate the briefing, raise the alert. Some belong to one role alone. Many do not.

  1. Strategy establishes what each new agent needs to be able to do, and which of it your roster can already do.
  2. Shared capability is built first, because each piece pays back across several agents at once.
  3. The next agent goes live sooner and with less risk, because part of the job is already built, already running and already audited.

Illustrative. The actual ratio of shared to role-specific capability is established during strategy, before anything is committed.

You subscribe per digital employee

No enterprise-wide license to sign before you've seen anyone work. You subscribe to the agents you've actually hired, monthly, and add to the roster when you're ready.

You approve every hire

Every digital employee is scoped, priced and approved individually against a fixed rate card, which we put in front of you before you commit to anything. You set the pace and the headcount.

You can stop at any point

Your existing team keeps working. There's no cliff, no all-or-nothing commitment, and no eighteen-month leap of faith.

What a digital employee costs

One-time build

A fixed fee per agent to configure it to your process, integrate it and put it into production. Flat, not hourly, so the scope risk sits with us rather than with you.

Monthly subscription

A per-agent fee for as long as it works for you, set by scope and complexity. This is the majority of what you pay us, and it is the part you can stop.

Across the first year the build is the minority of the cost by design: never more than a quarter of it. We put the full rate card in front of you before you commit to anything, and every agent is priced from it rather than negotiated case by case.

FAQs

Common questions.

Are you a platform company or a consultancy?+
A platform company. AyattAI's revenue is recurring subscription against digital employees running in production, and services are held deliberately below a quarter of the total. The three services exist because software alone does not change how an organization works: someone has to decide which work should become an agent, redesign the process around a mixed team, and train the people who will manage it. Those engagements are scoped to finish. The platform is what you are left holding, and it is what you keep paying for.
What is AyattAI?+
AyattAI is an enterprise agentic AI platform. It deploys cross-functional AI agents across CXO roles and business value streams, connecting goals, tasks, data, and decisions so AI agents collaborate and deliver measurable business outcomes.
What does āyatt mean?+
Āyatt is derived from the Sanskrit word Svāyatt, meaning self-governing, autonomous, and independent. Pronounced eye-uht, it embodies AyattAI's vision of AI systems that act with intelligence and autonomy while remaining under human supervision and governance.
What is the difference between Helm and Flow?+
Helm is the agent platform. Flow is the workflow orchestration engine inside Helm. Helm holds the agents, organized by role, one per function such as HR, Legal, Finance or Operations. Flow does not have agents of its own; it sequences Helm's agents into end-to-end value streams like Hire to Retire, Order to Cash and Procure to Pay, with human approval gates where they matter. The same HR agent that answers an employee question is the one Flow calls in step two of Hire to Retire.
What are Data Prism and Strata?+
They are AyattAI's other three platforms. Data Prism is the data lake and semantic layer. It turns scattered enterprise records into business meaning that agents can reason over, through semantic models, a business ontology, a knowledge graph and source mapping. Strata is the machine learning layer, adding forecasting, anomaly detection, segmentation and recommendation on top of that understanding. Helm runs the agents; Data Prism gives them meaning; Strata gives them foresight.
What is Matrix, the Digital Chief of Staff?+
A Digital Chief of Staff is an AI agent that continuously observes the entire enterprise, monitors health signals across every function, surfaces risks and priorities to leadership, and coordinates cross-functional action without waiting to be asked. AyattAI Matrix is the first enterprise Digital Chief of Staff.
How long does it take to deploy AyattAI?+
Twelve weeks is what we commit to for a first digital employee working in production on a real process, and it is what our automotive deployment actually took. Simpler scopes finish sooner: a single agent against a clean data source can be live in four to six weeks. Larger scopes take longer, and we tell you before you sign rather than after. The variable is almost never the model. It is how many systems have to be reconciled and how ready the data inside them is, which is exactly what the strategy phase establishes before anything is committed.
Who is AyattAI designed for?+
AyattAI is designed for mid-market and enterprise organizations ready to move beyond AI tools and deploy AI as an operating layer, particularly those with complex cross-functional operations, multiple CXO roles, and established enterprise systems like ERP, CRM, and HRIS.
